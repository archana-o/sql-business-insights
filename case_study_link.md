
# What I Learned from 10 SQL Business Problems

When I started this project, my goal wasn't just to practice SQL syntax. I wanted to understand how businesses use data to make decisions.

As I worked through these 10 SQL analyses, I realized that every query answered a different business question. SQL isn't just about retrieving data—it's about uncovering insights that help businesses make better decisions.

For example, I learned that **revenue alone doesn't tell the whole story**. A company can have increasing sales while also experiencing higher cancellations and refunds, which may indicate operational issues or poor customer experience.

The **customer retention** analysis showed me that acquiring new customers is only the beginning. Sustainable growth depends on how many customers return and continue purchasing over time.

Working on the **marketing funnel** and **attribution** queries helped me understand that not every marketing channel contributes in the same way. Some channels are better at attracting new customers, while others are more effective at converting them into paying customers.

I also discovered that **product performance should be measured using net revenue rather than gross sales**. Products with high return or refund rates can negatively impact overall profitability, even if they generate significant sales.

Finally, analyses like **payment failures**, **shipping performance**, and **customer lifetime value (LTV)** reinforced that business success depends on much more than revenue. Every stage of the customer journey—from payment to delivery to repeat purchases—plays a role in creating a positive customer experience and driving long-term growth.

## Final Thoughts

This project changed the way I think about SQL. Instead of viewing it as a language for writing queries, I now see it as a tool for solving business problems. Each analysis helped me understand how data can be transformed into actionable insights that support better decision-making.

More importantly, I learned that asking the **right business questions** is just as valuable as writing the SQL to answer them.
